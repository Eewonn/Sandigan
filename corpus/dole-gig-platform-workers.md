DOLE Labor Advisory No. 09-21 acknowledges the rise of platform-based work arrangements (ride-hailing, food delivery, online freelancing, etc.) and clarifies the legal framework.

Employment status test (Four-Fold / Economic Dependence Test): A gig or platform worker may be considered an employee — not an independent contractor — if ALL of the following elements exist:
1. Selection and engagement by the platform/company.
2. Payment of wages or remuneration.
3. Power of dismissal (platform can deactivate or remove the worker).
4. Power of control over the means and methods of work (not just the result).

If the platform exercises substantial control over how you perform your work (e.g., mandates routes, scripts, ratings requirements, uniform appearance), you may be deemed a regular employee despite a "freelancer" or "partner" label in your contract.

Rights if deemed an employee: If the four-fold test is satisfied, the worker is entitled to all Labor Code benefits: minimum wage, overtime pay, holiday pay, 13th month pay, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG contributions, and security of tenure.

Rights regardless of employment status:
- Freedom from forced labor and hazardous working conditions (Constitution, Art. XIII).
- Right to form associations (though collective bargaining rights depend on employee status).
- Right to safe working conditions under DOLE's Occupational Safety and Health Standards (RA 11058).

Practical note: If a platform company has been deducting fees, setting fixed schedules, and requiring you to follow detailed operational procedures, you likely have an employer-employee relationship. File a Request for Assistance at the nearest DOLE Regional Office (SEnA process) to clarify your status and claim any unpaid benefits. You may also file a complaint directly with the NLRC.
