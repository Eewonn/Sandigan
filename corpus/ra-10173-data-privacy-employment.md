Section 12 (Criteria for Lawful Processing of Personal Information): An employer may process an employee's personal data only when: (a) the employee has given consent; (b) processing is necessary for the performance of the employment contract; (c) processing is required by law; or (d) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation.

Section 13 (Sensitive Personal Information): Health records, biometric data, and sexual life information are classified as sensitive personal information and require explicit written consent or a specific legal basis to process. Employers may not collect or retain these without proper justification.

Section 16 (Rights of the Data Subject / Employee):
- Right to be informed: employees must be told what personal data the employer collects and why.
- Right to access: employees may request a copy of their personal data held by the employer.
- Right to correction: employees may demand correction of inaccurate personal data.
- Right to object: employees may object to processing of their personal data where no legitimate basis exists.
- Right to erasure: employees may request deletion of personal data no longer necessary for the purpose it was collected.

Workplace surveillance: CCTV in the workplace is generally permissible for safety and security purposes, but employees must be notified of monitoring. Recording in restrooms, changing areas, or other private spaces is prohibited. Monitoring of employees' personal devices or communications without consent is a violation.

Practical note: If your employer shares your personal data (e.g., salary, health records, home address) with third parties without your consent, or requires biometric data without a clear legal basis, you may file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission (NPC) at privacy.gov.ph.
